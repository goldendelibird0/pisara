2Questions for the meeting

Settle these before the fund is shared. The board knows conditions the data does not.

  1. 19 schools need both reading help and more room. Are they first in line for next year's classroom and repair works?
  2. 9 schools need reading help but are not crowded. What does the fund pay for there now: materials, training, or remedial sessions?
  3. 38 schools read well but are crowded. Can classroom works there wait a year, or not?
  4. 3,178 learners are at the lowest reading level. In 2025, 100% of the fund's reported spending was charged to elementary schools (section 3). How much of next year's share will go to reading support, compared with buildings and operations?

3The money for these schools

Two kinds of public money reach Ormoc City's public elementary schools. The board decides one. DepEd sets the other by formula.

Decided by the board

Special Education Fund

₱33.7 million

spent in 2025, for schools at all levels. Collected from property tax: ₱96.2 million.

Can pay for running public schools, building and repairing classrooms, books, research and sports.

Set by DepEd

School MOOE (maintenance and operating budget)

₱79.2 million

allocated for 2026 to all 81 public elementary schools in Ormoc City.

Day-to-day running: supplies, utilities, minor repairs and upkeep. It does not pay for new classrooms.

The board cannot change MOOE. It decides what the fund adds. MOOE is shared by formula, mainly by enrolment, so aiming money at the 19 schools that need both reading help and more room is the board's choice, through the fund.

How to read this brief

  • Schools are compared with a regional figure, not ranked. Schools work in different conditions, and DepEd's terms of use do not allow ranking.
  • The groups point to a kind of help to look into. They do not show that crowding causes low reading, or that spending will fix it.
  • Reading results are for school year 2025-26 only and are not compared across years. Classroom counts are from 2023-24; a school with no classroom count is treated as not crowded.
  • Regional figures cover the schools in DepEd's public reading file: five regions, 6,113 schools.
  • † Fewer than 20 learners assessed. Percentages for small groups move a lot with one or two learners.
  • School data: Department of Education, Project Bukas machine-ready files and the FY 2026 school MOOE allocation, used under DepEd's Terms of Use and Fair Use Policy. Tax allotment data: DBM COMPASS public export. Special Education Fund data: Bureau of Local Government Finance (BLGF), FY2025 preliminary. This is an independent prototype.
