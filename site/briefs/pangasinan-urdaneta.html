2Questions for the meeting

Settle these before the fund is shared. The board knows conditions the data does not.

  1. 17 schools need both reading help and more room. Are they first in line for next year's classroom and repair works?
  2. 14 schools need reading help but are not crowded. What does the fund pay for there now: materials, training, or remedial sessions?
  3. 6 schools read well but are crowded. Can classroom works there wait a year, or not?
  4. 2,592 learners are at the lowest reading level. In 2025, 0% of the fund's reported spending was charged to elementary schools and 0% to general administration (section 3). How much of next year's fund goes to learning support, compared with buildings and operations?

For scale

For scale, City of Urdaneta's National Tax Allotment for 2027 is ₱1.18 billion, up 10.7% from 2026. That is the main national transfer to the locality and is not specific to education.

How to read this brief

  • Schools are compared with a regional figure, not ranked. Schools work in different conditions, and DepEd's terms of use do not allow ranking.
  • The groups point to a kind of help to look into. They do not show that crowding causes low reading, or that spending will fix it.
  • Reading results are for school year 2025-26 only and are not compared across years. Classroom counts are from 2023-24; a school with no classroom count is treated as not crowded.
  • Regional figures cover the schools in DepEd's public reading file: five regions, 6,113 schools.
  • † Fewer than 20 learners assessed. Percentages for small groups move a lot with one or two learners.
  • School data: Department of Education, Project Bukas machine-ready files, used under DepEd's Terms of Use and Fair Use Policy. Tax allotment data: DBM COMPASS public export. Special Education Fund data: Bureau of Local Government Finance (BLGF), FY2025 preliminary. This is an independent prototype. It is not produced, reviewed or endorsed by DepEd, DBM or BLGF.
