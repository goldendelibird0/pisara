    How it works

    For the Local School Board: the mayor, the schools division superintendent and the other members who decide how the Special Education Fund is shared.

    Behind the scenes

    PISARA does the groundwork no board has time for

    Every brief starts from data the government already publishes. PISARA turns it into something a board can act on, and keeps it current.

    • Gathersthe public files from DepEd, DBM and BLGF, so no one has to hunt for them
    • Connectsevery school's reading results, enrolment, teachers and classrooms into one record
    • Compareseach school with others in its region, fairly and without ranking
    • Explainswhat it finds in plain language, and rebuilds whenever new data is out
    1. Open your city or town's briefSearch above. Each brief covers every public elementary school in the place.
    2. See which schools need whatEvery school sits in one of four groups: reading help, more classrooms, both, or neither, with what the board could do for each.
    3. Settle the questionsThe brief ends with the questions to settle before the fund is split, so the discussion starts from the same facts.

    Works today

    Built and checked against published data

    • A brief for 256 cities and municipalities in five regions, covering 6,041 public elementary schools
    • Four groups of schools, with what the board could do for each and the questions to settle
    • What each city or town collected for its Special Education Fund in 2025, and what the spending was charged to
    • Every brief rebuilt from the published files with one command
    • The same content as open data: schools, localities, JSON, dictionary
